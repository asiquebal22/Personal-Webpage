RL essentials and credit assignment

What is actually optimized when a robot learns from experience?

Critical14–18 study hoursConnected lab 01 →

What to understand

  • MDPs versus POMDPs; return, discount, occupancy and the difference between state and observation.
  • Bellman expectation and optimality; Monte Carlo versus TD; bootstrapping, bias and variance.
  • Policy gradients, baselines, advantage estimation and actor–critic methods.
  • Exploration, replay, reward design, reward hacking, sparse success signals and termination versus time-limit truncation.

What to derive or explain

  1. Write and solve a small Bellman system; check the Bellman residual.
  2. Derive REINFORCE from the likelihood-ratio identity and show why a state-dependent baseline preserves its expectation.
  3. Calculate GAE by hand on a short trajectory, distinguishing a terminal state from a truncated rollout.

Connect it to code

Solve a tiny blocked gridworld exactly, then add a new wall while keeping the goal fixed. Compare evaluating the old policy with replanning the policy. Use the included Bellman example before a neural implementation.

Interview retrieval practice

  • Why can a policy have high training return yet fail under a small obstruction?
  • What is the bias–variance trade-off in TD learning?
  • How does partial observability change your agent design?

Mastery check

Derive the Bellman equation and policy gradient unaided; produce a reproducible tabular result and explain the Dyna learning/planning loop. Use Sutton & Barto chapters 3–6/13 or Spinning Up for a first explanation, not as two complete parallel courses.
