Learned world models and imagination

What must a model predict for imagined training to improve physical behavior?

Research core16–20 study hoursConnected lab 08 →

What to understand

  • Action-conditioned transition models; state, image and latent predictions; recurrent belief states under partial observation.
  • RSSM-style posterior versus prior, reconstruction and predictive losses, reward and continuation heads.
  • Dreamer-style actor learning in imagined rollouts versus DINO-WM-style planning with learned visual features.
  • Compounding error, exposure bias, rollout horizon, model exploitation and the difference between a realistic video and useful dynamics.

What to derive or explain

  1. Write p(z[t+1] | z[t], a[t]) and an observation-conditioned posterior, explaining what is available at deployment.
  2. Explain the components of a latent world-model loss and what each can fail to preserve.
  3. Draw the computational graph for a frozen world model and a trainable actor; specify whether learning uses differentiable rollouts or a synthetic-transition replay buffer.

Connect it to code

Fit a small ensemble to blocked and unblocked transitions. Evaluate multi-step errors and success/failure discrimination, not just one-step loss. Add a frozen visual encoder only after the state-based diagnostic is working; make clear which result uses which observations.
