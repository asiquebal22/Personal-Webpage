06 / Interview preparation

Be able to explain the whole system.

Prepare from day one; begin outreach only after six full weeks. Combine fundamentals, coding, research judgment and evidence from your own implementation.

Outreach starts in Week 7: . Weeks 1–6 are private study, coding, project work and rehearsal. This website does not send messages or create scheduled outreach.

The career preparation sequence

Weeks 1–2

Private preparation

Assess gaps, collect existing research stories and write a focused one-page CV outline. Practice coding and whiteboard derivations. No outreach or applications yet.

Weeks 3–4

Build evidence

Prepare a concise account of your strongest prior work and a private baseline demo. Map robot-learning, world-model and evaluation roles to your actual strengths and gaps.

Weeks 5–6

Rehearse privately

Create a two-minute project explanation, a ten-minute talk and a list of suitable teams. Conduct private mocks. Finish the contact list and draft messages, but wait until Week 7 to send them.

Weeks 7–8

Outreach starts

After six full weeks, begin a small tailored batch of researcher conversations and applications. Share clearly labeled preliminary work. Track role fit and follow-ups; increase interview practice.

Weeks 9–10

Research and systems interviews

Practice technical deep dives, implementation/debugging and system design. Present actual ablations and negative results. Refine role-specific narratives using feedback.

Weeks 11–12

Interview intensively

Use the reproducible artifact, demo and report in research presentations. Focus review on recurring weaknesses from mocks and actual interviews. Keep future-work claims distinct from completed work.

Coding practice that supports the role

Transformer and adaptation mechanics
Write scaled dot-product attention with a temporal mask and a low-rank linear update W + BA. Check shapes and trainable-parameter counts. Use this only where prerequisites are weak, replacing lower-priority review within theory hours.
Tensor and data handling
Implement an episode-aware sequence sampler, padded masks and train-only normalization. Debug a batch×time×feature shape mismatch.
RL mechanics
Write a replay buffer, n-step/GAE targets, Polyak updates and a squashed-Gaussian log-probability. Explain tests for each.
Geometry and planning
Compose/invert poses, implement a two-link Jacobian and write CEM elite updates. Discuss unreachable goals and numerical stability.
Experimental analysis
Compute grouped success rates, paired bootstrap intervals and a ranking score without accessing actual candidate labels.
Software fundamentals
Practice Python collections, complexity, array/vectorization, clean APIs and debugging. Add role-specific data-structure exercises rather than replacing technical research practice with generic puzzles.

34 questions to answer with evidence

Answer aloud before expanding the rubric. Use equations, dimensions, one concrete example and one limitation.

01 / RL
Derive a policy gradient and explain a baseline.
A strong answer should cover: Start from expected trajectory return, use the score-function identity, state the assumptions, then explain which variance the baseline removes. Connect to delayed success after moving a blocker.
02 / RL
Explain PPO versus SAC for robot learning.
A strong answer should cover: Discuss on/off-policy data use, clipping, entropy, action distributions, sample efficiency and stability. Tie the choice to expensive physical samples and synthetic replay.
03 / RL
How do termination and time-limit truncation affect targets?
A strong answer should cover: Distinguish true terminal states from artificial rollout boundaries. Explain bootstrapping and how an incorrect done mask can teach the robot that timeouts are terminal failures.
04 / RL
Why does offline RL suffer from optimistic action values?
A strong answer should cover: Explain distribution shift in actions and bootstrapped critic targets. Give a concrete case where the actor chooses an unsupported action because its Q estimate is wrong.
05 / RL
What would you inspect when training does not improve?
A strong answer should cover: Start with environment/action/reward correctness and a small overfit test. Then inspect observations, masks, targets, gradients, entropy and update-to-data ratio. Avoid proposing hyperparameter search first.
06 / Robotics
How do you convert a camera observation into a robot command?
A strong answer should cover: Describe calibration, pose estimation, coordinate transforms, action representation, low-level control and timing. Mention which information is measured rather than assumed.
07 / Robotics
What does a Jacobian tell you, and what happens at a singularity?
A strong answer should cover: Explain task-space velocity mapping, rank loss and damped least-squares IK. Use a two-link numerical example.
08 / Robotics
How do position and impedance control differ at contact?
A strong answer should cover: Describe how commanded motion and compliance interact with unknown surfaces. Connect to safe execution and why a high-level action does not uniquely determine contact outcome.
09 / Robotics
Why can robot learning work in simulation and fail on hardware?
A strong answer should cover: Separate perception, calibration, timing, geometry, material/contact and controller mismatch. Propose measurements that distinguish them.
10 / Robotics
How would you design a repeatable manipulation evaluation?
A strong answer should cover: Specify resets, task rubric, layout families, method order, calibration, logging, aborts, independent outcomes and operator overhead.
11 / Policies
Why use an action chunk instead of one action?
A strong answer should cover: Explain multimodality, temporal coherence and inference cost, then discuss latency and response to a newly introduced blocker.
12 / Policies
Compare behavior cloning, diffusion policy and a VLA.
A strong answer should cover: Separate learning objective, action distribution, conditioning information and deployment interface. A VLA is not automatically an explicit dynamics model.
13 / Policies
When should you fine-tune an adapter rather than an entire model?
A strong answer should cover: Reason from data, memory, inference budget, representation mismatch and retention. Describe what is frozen and how you would verify that choice.
14 / Policies
What makes a good robot-learning dataset?
A strong answer should cover: Discuss coverage of failures/contact, embodiment, timing, executed action fidelity, split integrity, metadata and deployment distribution—not just sample count.
15 / World models
What is a world model in your project?
A strong answer should cover: Give the learned action-conditioned transition, its inputs/outputs, training data and rollout use. Distinguish it from the physics engine, a symbolic planner and the policy.
16 / World models
Why is one-step prediction error insufficient?
A strong answer should cover: Explain compounding error, decision relevance and failure discrimination. Describe an obstacle that remains visible but does not constrain predicted motion.
17 / World models
How does Dreamer differ from visual MPC?
A strong answer should cover: Explain learned latent state and imagined actor learning versus test-time action-sequence optimization. Specify which parameters update and when.
18 / World models
What changes after an unexpected obstacle appears?
A strong answer should cover: First infer the current scene/belief. Decide whether model predictions are wrong or the policy is poorly matched. Model-state inference, model-weight correction and policy adaptation are distinct operations.
19 / World models
How can the policy exploit its simulator?
A strong answer should cover: Describe unsupported actions, optimistic rewards or impossible contact transitions. Explain why shared weights are not necessary for exploitation and why ensemble agreement is not proof of correctness.
20 / World models
Would you use longer imagined rollouts?
A strong answer should cover: Discuss reachable-state coverage, contact error, value bootstrapping and compute. Propose a measured horizon ablation with executed outcomes.
21 / Research
Why fine-tune instead of replanning?
A strong answer should cover: Name the same-model MPC baseline. Explain when persistent behavior changes and transfer across repeated encounters could amortize training cost. Accept that a detour may only need planning.
22 / Research
What exactly is new relative to RISE, FOSP, Fail2Progress and verification-based WAM improvement?
A strong answer should cover: State the verified overlap first, including Cosmos Policy and recent work such as EVO-WAM. Compare update timing, imagined experience selection, physical verification, data budget and planning baselines. Novelty remains a literature-backed claim, not an assumption.
23 / Research
How do you choose the next physical trial?
A strong answer should cover: Specify the candidate pool, score computed before execution, diversity, budget and random baseline. Explain a concrete failure of uncertainty sampling.
24 / Research
How do you know the model became more honest?
A strong answer should cover: Use optimism gap together with Brier score/discrimination and executed success. Explain why predicting failure for everything is not useful improvement.
25 / Research
How do you prevent evaluation leakage?
A strong answer should cover: Separate training, development/acquisition, final layouts and retention. Explain where thresholds, checkpoints and stopping rules were selected.
26 / Research
What would falsify your paper's headline?
A strong answer should cover: Name matched MPC/random-correction baselines and retention checks. Describe a negative outcome that would change your conclusion rather than your benchmark.
27 / Systems
Design the deployed adaptation service.
A strong answer should cover: Cover timestamped observations, blockage detection, checkpoint/model versions, rollout generation, time cap, verification, execution, rollback and logging. Count total recovery latency.
28 / Systems
How would you profile the training loop?
A strong answer should cover: Separate predictor calls, encoding, data transfer, rollout generation, updates, synchronization and checkpoint overhead. Report throughput and peak memory with the actual device.
29 / Systems
How do you reproduce a rare physical failure?
A strong answer should cover: Preserve the complete scene, calibration, seeds, model/policy hashes, raw observations and commanded/executed actions. Explain remaining nondeterminism.
30 / Career
Why move from academia into Physical AI?
A strong answer should cover: Connect existing modeling, uncertainty and research leadership strengths to specific deployed robot-learning problems. Use the artifact as evidence of new robotics/RL execution ability.
31 / World models
Trace the lineage from Dyna to modern world-action models.
A strong answer should cover: Distinguish tabular model backups, compressed learned simulation, PETS/PlaNet planning, Dreamer actor learning, MBPO short synthetic replay, and pretrained video/action modeling. Explain which ideas your project actually implements.
32 / Policies
How do original OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT and π₀ differ?
A strong answer should cover: Compare pretrained backbones, observations, original discrete action tokens, continuous chunked decoding and flow-based action generation. Explain dataset-specific normalization, LoRA versus full updates, inference latency and the exact checkpoint you inspected or ran.
33 / World models
How would you use Cosmos in this robot adaptation project?
A strong answer should cover: Identify exact Cosmos version/checkpoint and whether it provides reasoning, video generation, action-conditioned forward dynamics or robot actions. Compare Cosmos Policy and DreamZero. Specify data/embodiment compatibility, throughput, policy update objective and independent executed evaluation.
34 / World models
What distinguishes DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC from a generic video model?
A strong answer should cover: Explain feature prediction versus pixel generation, representation pretraining versus action-conditioned robot post-training, and online planning versus persistent policy updates. Name information that a useful feature representation can still lose at contact.

Your project, at three depths

  • 2 minutes: physical problem, why a learned model helps, your method, one measured result and one limitation.
  • 10 minutes: setting, strongest related work, architecture, baseline fairness, main figures and failure analysis.
  • 30 minutes: equations, tensor/data flow, implementation decisions, ablations, statistical uncertainty, costs and next research steps.

Prepare three prior-research stories as well: a technical contribution, a difficult failure/debugging episode, and leadership/collaboration. Connect existing expertise to the new robot-learning evidence without implying that three months covers every robotics specialty.
