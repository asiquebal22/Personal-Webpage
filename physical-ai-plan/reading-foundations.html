Reading room / Foundational papers
Foundations / A bounded reading curriculum

The ideas behind today’s models.

A curated historical and technical progression, followed by modern extensions. The twelve Core papers are required deep reads; Survey and Reference items have different purposes.

Why this belongs in the plan: industry fluency requires understanding where the algorithms came from, what changed, and why a particular method fits the robot. The project uses a manageable implementation; the knowledge curriculum covers the wider field.

Three connected lines of development

Models and decisions

Dyna → World Models → PETS / PlaNet → Dreamer / MBPO

Learn dynamics, then distinguish planning through them from learning a policy with them. Continue to DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC.

Robot policies and data

DAgger → ACT / Diffusion Policy → RT-1 / RT-2 → generalist VLAs

Study distribution shift, multimodal actions, scaling robot data and VLM transfer. Compare Open X-Embodiment, Octo, OpenVLA/OFT and π₀.

Foundation world and action models

Transformers / generative models → Cosmos → Cosmos Policy / DreamZero

Connect representation and generation to actual robot actions. Study the current Cosmos 3 architecture and checkpoint interfaces.

These are conceptual learning paths, not claims that each paper directly descends from its neighbor or that one family supersedes another.

Read in this order

A two-hour deep pass includes the objective/architecture, the relevant experiment and a one-page note. A 20–30 minute survey establishes the mechanism and limitations. If a prerequisite is new, replace a lower-priority survey with a deeper pass. Reading hours below are already inside the existing theory allocation.

Weeks 1–6: twelve foundational/core papers. Weeks 7–8: four close project papers. Weeks 9–12: retrieval, novelty checks, writing and targeted review. Paper reading is one component of theory time; derivations and exercises use the remainder.

The annotated paper shelf

Core: read deeply and explain the objective. Survey: understand the architecture, setting and limitation. Reference: consult for a concrete gap; not another mandatory assignment. Foundational significance and current benchmark strength are different judgments.

Core / Model-based RL / 1991
First Results with Dyna, an Integrated Architecture for Learning, Planning and Reacting

Richard S. Sutton

Mechanism: Interleave learning from interaction with planning updates using a learned model.

What you should do with it: Draw the real-data/model-data/update loop. Extend the gridworld with a learned tabular transition table and compare equal real-transition budgets.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Model-based RL / 2018
World Models

David Ha & Jürgen Schmidhuber

Mechanism: A compressed visual representation and recurrent dynamics support a compact controller; a policy can be trained in the learned dream environment.

What you should do with it: Explain why imagined policy training predates current video foundation models, and why transfer back to the actual environment must be measured.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Learning / 2017
Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms

Schulman et al.

Mechanism: Optimize a clipped policy-surrogate objective using collected trajectories and repeated minibatch updates.

What you should do with it: Derive the clipped cases for both advantage signs and connect them to the supplied PPO update. Clipping does not guarantee model fidelity.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Learning / 2018
Soft Actor-Critic: Off-Policy Maximum Entropy Deep Reinforcement Learning with a Stochastic Actor

Haarnoja et al.

Mechanism: Learn a stochastic continuous-action actor and critics with an entropy-regularized objective and replay.

What you should do with it: Trace the practical SAC variant you run. Separate changes between the original paper and a modern automatic-temperature implementation.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Model-based RL / 2018
Deep Reinforcement Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models

Chua et al.

Mechanism: PETS combines probabilistic dynamics ensembles, trajectory sampling and action-sequence optimization.

What you should do with it: Justify the ensemble and MPC baseline. Ensemble spread is a diagnostic, not a calibrated guarantee against shared contact errors.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Model-based RL / 2018 / ICML 2019
Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels

Hafner et al.

Mechanism: PlaNet learns recurrent stochastic latent dynamics from images and plans within that latent space.

What you should do with it: Draw deterministic/stochastic state paths and posterior/prior inputs. Contrast latent-state inference with model-weight adaptation.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Model-based RL / 2019 / ICLR 2020
Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination

Hafner et al.

Mechanism: Dreamer learns an actor and value function through imagined latent trajectories.

What you should do with it: Contrast persistent actor updates with PlaNet's online action search. Trace which gradients pass through the model and which parameters are frozen.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Model-based RL / 2019
When to Trust Your Model: Model-Based Policy Optimization

Janner et al.

Mechanism: Short model-generated rollouts branch from real replay to limit the effect of model error during policy optimization.

What you should do with it: Design real/synthetic replay provenance and a rollout-horizon ablation. This is a direct foundation for the compact research loop.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Robot policies / 2023
Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion

Chi et al.

Mechanism: Represent a conditional distribution over robot action sequences through denoising and execute with receding-horizon control.

What you should do with it: Implement the two-route denoiser. Distinguish diffusion steps, prediction horizon, execution horizon and the control rate.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Robot policies / 2024
OpenVLA: An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model

Kim et al.

Mechanism: Adapt a pretrained vision-language backbone to predict tokenized robot actions using heterogeneous robot demonstrations.

What you should do with it: Trace image/instruction preprocessing, token-to-action decoding and dataset-specific action unnormalization. Study the limits of zero-shot embodiment transfer.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / Robot policies / 2024 / RSS 2025
π₀: A Vision-Language-Action Flow Model for General Robot Control

Black et al.

Mechanism: Combine a pretrained VLM with a flow-matching action expert for continuous robot action generation.

What you should do with it: Compare OpenVLA's original action tokens with flow-based action chunks. Explain the role of language conditioning versus a predictive dynamics model.

Read the primary paper ↗

Core / World-action models / 2026
Cosmos Policy: Fine-Tuning Video Models for Visuomotor Control and Planning

Kim et al.

Mechanism: Post-train a video foundation model to generate robot action chunks, future states and values; predictions can support test-time planning.

What you should do with it: Trace direct-policy versus planning deployment. This is essential related work, but its planning result does not establish deployment-time RL inside imagination.

What counts as understanding a paper?

  1. Draw the observations, actions, model, actor and learning signals, marking what is learned versus supplied.
  2. Write the main objective and explain each term, including where gradients stop.
  3. Locate one result that supports the central claim and one limitation relevant to your robot.
  4. Connect the paper to a working exercise or a precise implementation inspection.
  5. Explain what changed relative to its predecessor and what your project still needs to demonstrate.

This page provides study prompts and verified primary links. It does not claim that every paper has been reproduced or that abstracts establish detailed experimental validity. Use the six-question note for full-paper reading.
