05 / Reading room

Read for mechanisms and evidence.

A prioritized reading map. Neural world models, structured skill-effect models, symbolic planning and physics-based systems are labeled separately.

A repeatable 60–90 minute pass

The six-question paper note

  1. Setting: what is known, what changes, and what data/interaction are allowed?
  2. Model: what is learned, predicted or supplied by a simulator?
  3. Update: which parameters change, and is adaptation at training time or deployment?
  4. Evidence: which exact experiment demonstrates the behavior? Locate the figure, section and hardware/simulation distinction.
  5. Comparison: which baseline, budget, split and sample count support the claim?
  6. Transfer: what can we reproduce or reuse, and what limitation matters for our project?

Read two papers deeply per week early in the program; scan additional titles only to answer a specific gap. By Weeks 9–12, prioritize writing and evidence over an expanding reading backlog.

The project reading sequence

Weeks 1 / 3↗
Foundational physical world-model RL

DayDreamer · physical robot learning

Read to answer: How are robot experience, world-model fitting and imagined policy learning interleaved?

Evidence boundary: Real robots, but not the specific unexpected-obstruction recovery protocol. Reconstruct the learning loop and real-data requirements.

Weeks 2 / 5 / 7↗
Closest obstacle-adaptation baseline

FOSP · paper and robot adaptation

Read to answer: Which parameters are updated and which real interactions are still required?

Evidence boundary: Real-arm reaching with changed obstacles and offline-to-online learning. Check the paper's visual-generalization and dynamic-obstacle limitations; do not describe it as pure dream-only recovery.

Weeks 3 / 4↗
Neural predictive model + planning

DINO-WM · visual predictive models

Read to answer: What does the action-conditioned predictor output, and how does planning use it?

Evidence boundary: A planning reference, not automatic evidence that policy weights improve. Inspect visual-feature dynamics and evaluation interfaces.

Weeks 3–6↗
Architecture / implementation reference

DreamerV3 · reference repository

Read to answer: Which losses train perception, dynamics, reward and policy?

Evidence boundary: Separate the full algorithm from a compact teaching implementation. Record the exact source revision and environment used.

Weeks 3 / 4↗
Behavior reference: remove a blocker

RePLan · obstruction-driven replanning

Read to answer: How does a blocked subtask cause an extra manipulation step?

Evidence boundary: LLM/VLM replanning with MuJoCo, rather than a learned neural simulator. Read CabinetBlocked, KitchenExplore and the real-robot bowl example.

Weeks 4 / 8 / 9↗
Predictive-world-model failure under obstacles

Reimagination · obstacle-related prediction failures

Read to answer: Can unfamiliar objects disappear in rollouts and make failed actions look successful?

Evidence boundary: Real-robot plan verification; the study has ten trials per method. It selects plans rather than learning an unblocking skill. Inspect the post-hoc obstacle reinsertion assumption.

Weeks 4 / 5 / 9↗
Policy improvement in a neural world model

RISE · imagination-based policy improvement

Read to answer: How do imagined outcomes yield policy-training signals?

Evidence boundary: Check warm-up data, task-specific model training, compute, failure sensitivity and inference/training separation. Not the same as a brief event-triggered deployment update.

Weeks 6–8↗
Targeted correction of learned skill effects

Fail2Progress · targeted model correction

Read to answer: How are failure-conditioned simulation examples generated, and what is learned?

Evidence boundary: Structured predictive models; the constrained-shelf unblocking example is qualitative. Distinguish generated simulator data from actively chosen physical trials.

Weeks 6 / 8↗
Recovery skills + abstract world model

ReSYNC · recovery skills and planning concepts

Read to answer: Does failure cause learning a new skill, a concept, or a new composition?

Evidence boundary: Read curriculum/resettable-model assumptions and sim-to-real training details. Strong obstruction behavior, but not a video-world-model adaptation method.

Optional depth↗
Dreamer-based obstruction adaptation setup

Concept Bottleneck World Models · dissertation

Read to answer: What is the barrier novelty and how is adaptation evaluated?

Evidence boundary: Dissertation, not a standalone peer-reviewed paper. Appendix 8.12 includes unstacking a plate; aggregate adaptation curves do not isolate success on that case.

Optional depth↗
Physics-based strategy switching

NAMO · learned dynamic constraints

Read to answer: Why does the robot switch from pushing one table to manipulating another?

Evidence boundary: Learned dynamic constraints inside a physics model. Useful causal behavior example; not a modern neural world model.

Optional depth↗
Structured neural interaction prediction

Pushing It Out of the Way

Read to answer: How is a route forced to require moving an obstacle?

Evidence boundary: Simulation with obstacle-clearing tasks in training. Distinguish learned generalization from zero-shot invention of obstacle removal.

Keep a living novelty matrix

For each close paper, track learned model type, policy-update timing, obstruction novelty, model correction, real-trial selection, real-data budget, planning baseline and hardware evidence. The absence of a feature in an abstract does not establish novelty. Recheck the literature in Weeks 6 and 9 and before submission.

Promising comparison: event-triggered policy adaptation with actively selected physical correction. This remains a research hypothesis until the closest methods and their appendices have been checked.
